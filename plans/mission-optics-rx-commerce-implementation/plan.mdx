# Mission Optics Rx Commerce Platform — Shopify-First Implementation Plan

## Objective

A shopper on a sunglass brand’s product page selects **Add prescription lenses**, completes a Mission-hosted lens configuration, and checks out through the brand’s existing Shopify cart. Shopify remains authoritative for payment, tax, discounts, refunds, and the commercial order. Mission remains authoritative for optical eligibility, prescription records, lens pricing logic, manufacturing instructions, lab release, remakes, and fulfillment.

The first release must prove this complete journey with one pilot brand and a bounded catalog while establishing reusable contracts for future WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Commerce Layer, and headless adapters.

<Callout id="architecture-decision" tone="decision">

**Committed architecture:** build the Mission Rx service first and Shopify as
an adapter. Core Rx types must contain no Shopify-specific identifiers.
Prescription values and documents never enter commerce metadata, storefront
analytics, or ordinary logs.

</Callout>

## Scope and non-goals

### In scope for the first production pilot

- Single Shopify brand and one primary market/currency.
- Approximately 10–25 frame variants and a deliberately bounded lens catalog.
- Plano versus Rx choice, prescription upload/manual-entry workflow, PD collection, compatible lens options, price, and customer-safe review.
- Theme App Extension, hosted configurator, native Shopify cart lines/components, checkout validation, order capture, cancellation/refund synchronization, fulfillment writeback, and tracking.
- Merchant setup for store connection, catalog mapping, price-book assignment, brand policy, and support diagnostics.
- Reliable event processing, reconciliation, quarantine, audit, and operational runbooks.

### Explicitly deferred

- A unique commerce variant for every prescription combination.
- Multi-platform production launch before the Shopify pilot passes.
- Advanced insurance adjudication, patient benefits, or payer billing.
- Automated manufacturing release for cases that require optician review.
- Full global markets, multi-currency, localization, and tax-policy expansion.
- Consumer virtual try-on and digital-twin work, except for carrying stable frame identifiers needed later.

<RichText id="workstream-zero-heading" title={"Workstream 0 — Product, optical, and commercial contracts"}>

## Workstream 0 — Product, optical, and commercial contracts

**Owners:** product/program lead and optical/Rx domain lead. **Timing:** Days 1–15.

### Committed financial comparison basis

Mission quote lines represent gross frame/lens/service merchandise amounts in the checkout currency **before Shopify discounts, tax, and shipping**. The adapter compares each bound commercial component SKU, quantity, and gross unit amount to the signed execution plan. Shopify remains authoritative for discount allocation, tax allocation, shipping, payment fees, refunds, and final captured total; Mission records those allocations separately rather than requiring the whole Shopify order total to equal the pre-tax quote.

### Committed Shopify catalog ownership

The Mission app provisions or verifies a finite set of hidden lens/service products and variants per merchant. Mission owns the intended SKU mapping, component role, expected price, shipping requirement, and configuration digest. The merchant approves tax and discount policy. A scheduled health check detects edits, deletions, publication mistakes, or price drift and disables new Rx checkout until repaired.

</RichText>

<RichText id="model-heading" title="Canonical data model">

## Canonical data model

All customer and merchant records are tenant-scoped. Configuration, prescription, quote, cart, order, and fulfillment keep separate state machines and revisions. The schema is additive and provider-neutral.

A single `RxConfiguration` represents **one physical pair** and cart quantity must remain one. A shopper requesting two pairs receives two configuration IDs, even when selections are identical.

`rx_configurations.selections` contains only typed, non-prescription commercial lens choices. Sphere, cylinder, axis, add, prism, PD, segment height, documents, and patient identifiers are structurally confined to the prescription domain.

Rules, price books, manufacturing instructions, and customer-safe summaries are stored as immutable append-only snapshots or content-addressed artifacts—not mutable records labeled with an old version.

Required database constraints include unique provider delivery per store, unique external order binding per store, one active cart binding per quote revision, one fulfillment case per order package, one lab-job effect per fulfillment revision, and one outbox event per aggregate revision/event type. Idempotency records also bind request key to body digest and original result.

</RichText>

<RichText id="adapter-heading" title="Commerce adapter contract">

## Commerce adapter contract

The adapter is split between a storefront bridge and an authenticated administration adapter. Unsupported capabilities must resolve to an explicit fallback or installation failure; core code does not branch on provider names.

The final contract must additionally cover validation-token materialization and limits, cart package normalization, order edits, holds/releases, cancellation and refund normalization, fulfillment splits, capability-version provenance, and plan-tier restrictions. Replace ambiguous booleans with strategy enums where behavior differs materially.

Before calling the core platform-neutral, implement an executable fake non-Shopify adapter and run the complete conformance suite against it. A minimal WooCommerce contract spike after the Shopify pilot is the first cross-language proof.

</RichText>

## Workstream 2 — Shopify reference adapter

**Owners:** Shopify engineer and backend engineer. **Timing:** Days 16–60.

Ship the multi-merchant version as a public Shopify app, even if distribution is initially limited to invited brands. The product-page app block is the primary launcher; a global app embed provides the fallback for quick-add, cart drawers, and themes whose product section cannot host app blocks.

Checkout enforcement must not depend on a live call to Mission. The Cart and Checkout Validation Function verifies a self-contained signed cart token containing only store, case, allowed component IDs and quantities, configuration/catalog version, key ID, and expiry. The Days 1–7 spike must establish Function input materialization, payload limits, signature algorithm, key rotation overlap, and the exact fail-closed behavior when token data is missing or malformed. Mission continuously verifies that the merchant has activated the validation rule in fail-closed mode.

**Outage policy:** if Mission is unavailable, shoppers cannot start or change a configuration. A cart with an already-issued unexpired valid token can still pass Shopify’s local validation. Any paid order not yet captured by Mission remains quarantined and is recovered through webhook retry/reconciliation. Manufacturing never fails open.

## Workstream 5 — Lab fulfillment and operations

**Owners:** fulfillment engineer, optical lead, lab operations, support lead. **Timing:** Days 31–75.

A lab job may be created only when all release predicates are true: the authoritative Shopify order exists; the merchant-approved financial state is satisfied; fraud/risk and cancellation holds are clear; bound commercial lines match the signed plan; the prescription case is approved; the optical configuration is approved; frame/lens availability is confirmed; the latest order edit/refund/cancellation has been processed; and the manufacturing release policy permits work to begin. ## Post-pilot adapter roadmap

Before selecting adapter two, inventory the actual commerce platforms used by 100–200 qualified Mission prospects. Weight signed demand, GMV, geography, implementation uniformity, and support burden—not global store count alone.

1. **WooCommerce** when reachable merchant demand outweighs its plugin/theme/hosting variability.
2. **BigCommerce** when Mission values a cleaner SaaS adapter and mid-market/headless capability.
3. **Commerce Layer** for Mission-owned B2B, headless, spatial, or white-label channels where Mission intentionally owns the transaction path.
4. **Adobe Commerce or Salesforce Commerce** only for an anchor enterprise engagement that funds bespoke packaging and support.
5. **Wix and Squarespace** through the hosted configurator/fallback strategy when qualified demand justifies their more constrained surfaces.

The investment gate for any adapter is three committed design partners or one anchor account that covers build and first-year support.
