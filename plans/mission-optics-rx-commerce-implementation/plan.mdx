# Mission Optics Rx Commerce Platform — Shopify-First Implementation Plan

## Objective

A shopper on a sunglass brand’s product page selects **Add prescription lenses**, completes a Mission-hosted lens configuration, and checks out through the brand’s existing Shopify cart. Shopify remains authoritative for payment, tax, discounts, refunds, and the commercial order. Mission remains authoritative for optical eligibility, prescription records, lens pricing logic, manufacturing instructions, lab release, remakes, and fulfillment.

The first release must prove this complete journey with one pilot brand and a bounded catalog while establishing reusable contracts for future WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Commerce Layer, and headless adapters.

<Callout id="architecture-decision" tone="decision">

**Committed architecture:** build the Mission Rx service first and Shopify as
an adapter. Core Rx types must contain no Shopify-specific identifiers.
Prescription values and documents never enter commerce metadata, storefront
analytics, or ordinary logs.

</Callout>

## Scope and non-goals

### In scope for the first production pilot

- HyperLight (`https://hyperlightoptics.com/`) as the single Shopify pilot brand, with one United States market, USD pricing, domestic fulfillment, and adult Prescription Subjects only.
- An assignable Mission Pilot Program Owner role, with the initial HyperLight assignee TBA.
- A HyperLight Pilot Catalog Allowlist of approximately 12–15 adult, in-stock frame variants and a deliberately bounded lens catalog.
- Plano versus Rx choice; prescription document upload, independently verified manual entry, secure post-payment submission, and eligible saved-record reuse; PD collection; compatible lens options; price; and customer-safe review.
- Theme App Extension, hosted configurator, native Shopify cart lines/components, checkout validation, order capture, cancellation/refund synchronization, fulfillment writeback, and tracking.
- Merchant setup for store connection, catalog mapping, price-book assignment, brand policy, and support diagnostics.
- Reliable event processing, reconciliation, quarantine, audit, and operational runbooks.

### Explicitly deferred

- A unique commerce variant for every prescription combination.
- Multi-platform production launch before the Shopify pilot passes.
- Advanced insurance adjudication, patient benefits, or payer billing.
- Automated manufacturing release for cases that require optician review.
- Full global markets, multi-currency, localization, and tax-policy expansion.
- Consumer virtual try-on and digital-twin work, except for carrying stable frame identifiers needed later.

<RichText id="workstream-zero-heading" title={"Workstream 0 — Product, optical, and commercial contracts"}>

## Workstream 0 — Product, optical, and commercial contracts

**Owners:** Pilot Program Owner and optical/Rx domain lead. **Linear milestone:** M0 — Foundations approved.

### Committed financial comparison basis

Mission quote lines represent gross frame/lens/service merchandise amounts in the checkout currency **before Shopify discounts, tax, and shipping**. The adapter compares each bound commercial component SKU, quantity, and gross unit amount to the signed execution plan. Shopify remains authoritative for discount allocation, tax allocation, shipping, payment fees, refunds, and final captured total; Mission records those allocations separately rather than requiring the whole Shopify order total to equal the pre-tax quote.

An issued Rx Quote protects its base component prices until expiration. Merchant catalog changes affect only newly issued quotes; the adapter must honor an unexpired quote's locked prices, while approved Shopify discounts, tax, and shipping remain separate platform calculations. If a platform cannot safely honor the locked prices, checkout fails closed rather than charging different base amounts.

When the customer checks out before prescription approval, HyperLight collects the full quoted frame and selected lens-package price through Conditional Full Payment rather than a deposit. The customer sees that production is conditional on verification. A verified prescription that fits the paid package proceeds unchanged; a required higher-priced package creates a Change Proposal, and a lower-priced or unsupported outcome creates a Financial Adjustment Request for the commerce channel. Mission does not implement a separate deposit-collection workflow per platform.

For the HyperLight pilot, an accepted higher-price Change Proposal uses Shopify Order Editing to replace or add the affected unfulfilled lens/service components, commits the calculated edit, and then sends the purchaser Shopify's additional-payment collection URL or order invoice. Shopify recalculates authoritative tax and discount allocations. Mission re-fetches the edited order and withholds Financial Clearance and Production Release until the exact pair package and full additional payment are authoritative. Every adjustment is idempotent and binds the proposal, order revision, affected lines, amount, currency, and purchaser acceptance. Abandoned, expired, or failed collection remains on financial hold. If the order is not safely editable or Shopify cannot produce a collection path, the adapter makes no partial mutation and falls back to merchant-authorized cancellation/refund plus a replacement checkout.

For a lower-priced approved revision, the purchaser accepts the revised configuration before Mission sends a pair-specific Financial Adjustment Request. The commerce channel issues a partial refund to the original payment method by default and recalculates authoritative tax, discount, and payment allocations. Store credit is available only by explicit purchaser choice under approved merchant policy. Mission records the completed adjustment before Financial Clearance and Production Release; the price difference cannot be silently retained.

Rx Quotes expire after seven days by default. Merchants may choose a shorter approved window; longer windows require an approved commercial policy and remain bounded by a Mission maximum. Expiration is shown before cart creation, and the configurator attempts seamless re-quoting when the configuration and commercial terms have not changed.

### Committed Shopify catalog ownership

The Mission app provisions or verifies a finite set of hidden lens/service products and variants per merchant. Mission owns the intended SKU mapping, component role, expected price, shipping requirement, configuration digest, and approved promotion eligibility. Lens and service components are non-discountable by default; the merchant may enable only promotions permitted by its commercial agreement. A scheduled health check detects edits, deletions, publication mistakes, or price drift and disables new quote issuance until repaired without invalidating price protection for already-issued unexpired quotes.

Merchant operating choices are governed by a versioned Merchant Program Configuration containing the lens offering, price book, promotion rules, deadlines, communications, Delivery Policy, and permitted automated approvals. Draft changes pass sandbox validation before prospective publication. Mission approves optical, production, access, and safety-sensitive changes; merchants may publish permitted branding and commercial changes. Existing configurations, quotes, orders, and attempts remain pinned to their original effective version unless explicitly re-evaluated, and rollback republishes a known-good version without rewriting history.

Live Rx selling requires Store Certification. The connection must pass automated sandbox coverage and at least one end-to-end order through the merchant's real theme and enabled accelerated checkout paths, with catalog bindings, validation controls, permissions, webhooks, fulfillment, refunds, and reconciliation verified. Mission records its approval, merchant acceptance, and the certified capability fingerprint. Later drift that removes a required safety control suspends new configurations while preserving service for existing paid orders.

<RichText id="model-heading" title="Canonical data model">

## Canonical data model

Merchant operational records are tenant-scoped. A Merchant Organization is the canonical contracted tenant and may own multiple Store Connections across commerce platforms. Each connection has separate credentials, certification, capabilities, market, currency, catalog bindings, and external order namespace. Mission-owned Prescription Subjects, Subject Claims, Prescription Records, Measurement Records, and subject authorizations live in a separate subject security domain and never become merchant-owned rows. A merchant-scoped Prescription Case references those records only through an active, purpose-bound authorization for that pair. Organization-level templates may feed store-specific published configurations with permitted overrides. Authorized organization roles may receive cross-store commercial and operational reporting, but organization membership never grants protected optical access. Platform identifiers remain external bindings. Configuration, prescription, quote, cart, order, and fulfillment keep separate state machines and revisions. The schema is additive and provider-neutral.

Mission and merchant workforce users authenticate through Agent Native's Better Auth integration and framework-managed organizations. Anonymous shoppers begin through a short-lived Guest Capability derived from a verified HyperLight storefront context and bound to one store, configuration, and protected upload scope. A Mission Account is required only for ongoing subject access, saved-prescription reuse, or later record management, and guest data joins it only through an explicit Subject Claim. No request falls back to a shared anonymous account, and merchant membership never establishes subject authorization.

All Mission workforce users and merchant owners or administrators require MFA. Fresh Step-Up Authentication protects System Administrator publication, protected-data export, role or access-policy changes, Production Release, QA exceptions, and other designated high-consequence actions. Shoppers may use verified email access without permanent MFA but complete one-time identity verification for subject claims, prescription reuse, protected contact changes, and sensitive record actions. Agent-triggered high-consequence actions additionally require explicit human approval.

Pilot observability and audit data remain inside the isolated Neon data plane through Agent Native's built-in surfaces. Prompt content, action inputs and results, prescription data, documents, customer text, and commerce payloads are redacted by default; telemetry contains only opaque IDs, event and transition names, reason codes, durations, counts, and sanitized errors. External trace and analytics exporters remain disabled until provider, BAA, configuration, field, and retention review. Automated leak tests inject protected-looking values and block launch if they appear in logs, traces, analytics, alerts, or error reports.

The pilot targets a database recovery point of no more than 15 minutes and restoration of critical configuration, prescription-case, order, and fulfillment operations within four hours. Protected S3 objects use versioning and governed retention. Mission completes a database-and-document restore rehearsal before launch and repeats it quarterly and after material infrastructure changes. Restored state reconciles against Shopify, the Manual Pilot Queue, and immutable audit and event records before production reopens, and recovery credentials remain available outside the failed environment.

Mission workforce authentication targets verified Google Workspace SSO restricted to approved domains. For the HyperLight pilot only, individually assigned verified email-and-password Better Auth accounts are allowed when MFA is enabled; shared accounts and fallback identities remain prohibited. Merchant staff use separate verified Better Auth identities and MFA. Role removal revokes future access without altering historical attribution, and the pilot password exception does not define the general-availability policy.

The password exception ends before the second live merchant or general availability, whichever occurs first. At that gate, ordinary Mission password sign-in is disabled and at most two emergency accounts may remain under phishing-resistant MFA, offline recovery, immediate-use alerts, periodic review, and access testing.

Each pilot has one assignable Pilot Program Owner accountable for cross-functional readiness, merchant coordination, evidence collection, and go-live preparation. HyperLight's initial assignee is TBA. The role is not a shortcut to protected optical data or any separately governed approval, exception, release, QA, or System Administrator authority.

The Pilot Program Owner may view nonclinical pilot status, assign and escalate work, coordinate the go-live record, and immediately pause new HyperLight Rx configurations. Activation and resumption still require current product, optical, engineering, lab, merchant, security or privacy, and support approvals. Protected prescription access and all optical, exception, QA, and production-release powers require separately assigned roles.

The production pilot has one Approved Market: United States jurisdiction, USD pricing, and domestic fulfillment. Even in the pilot, records preserve jurisdiction, market, currency, tax allocations, prescribing-provider location, subject location, and fulfillment destination. International currencies, cross-border delivery, translated regulated content, and country-specific prescription rules remain unavailable until that market combination completes legal, optical, tax, privacy, content, and operational approval.

The pilot Subject Eligibility Policy accepts only Prescription Subjects aged 18 or older. Subject Representative support remains because adults may authorize assistance and because the same identity boundary can later support guardians. Minor support stays disabled until guardian authority and identity verification, consent, communications, prescription validity, fitting workflows, retention, and merchant policy receive separate approval.

HyperLight's Pilot Catalog Allowlist contains approximately 12–15 adult, in-stock variants chosen across representative constructions, materials, eye sizes, shapes, product positioning, lens replacement requirements, and production difficulty. Kids products, gift cards, standalone lens sets, clip-ons, sold-out items, and any frame without approved physical inventory and a Frame Optical Profile remain disabled for Rx even if they are published in the storefront.

A versioned Published Eligibility Envelope exposes the conservative customer-facing prescription range derived from frame, lens, lab, market, and merchant constraints. The system separately maintains independently validated Verified Production Capability. After a paid pair's actual prescription and pair-specific production facts are available, an admissions, customer-service, or lab-management user explicitly configured as an Rx Exception Manager may either initiate a compatible Configuration Rebase or Change Proposal, or issue a one-case Production Exception Approval outside the published range when the exact configuration remains inside verified capability. If the broader capability is not already documented, Mission first obtains technical or lab confirmation and publishes a new capability revision. The exception binds the reason, evidence, approver, timestamp, and every dependent revision, expires with the case, and never expands the catalog or overrides an unverified hard safety or manufacturing boundary.

Production Exception Approval and Optical Approval are separate decisions with separate Approval Bases. A qualified person may hold both Rx Exception Manager and Optical Reviewer roles but must perform and record each action independently; a customer-service manager without optical authority cannot approve the prescription or manufacturing interpretation. Production Release remains assigned to the separate Release Operator.

A single `RxConfiguration` represents **one physical pair** and cart quantity must remain one. A shopper requesting two pairs receives two configuration IDs, even when selections are identical.

`rx_configurations.selections` contains only typed, non-prescription commercial lens choices. Sphere, cylinder, axis, add, prism, PD, segment height, documents, and patient identifiers are structurally confined to the prescription domain.

Rules, price books, manufacturing instructions, and customer-safe summaries are stored as immutable append-only snapshots or content-addressed artifacts—not mutable records labeled with an old version.

Required database constraints include unique provider delivery per store, one canonical Commerce Order per external order reference within a Store Connection, one active cart binding per quote revision, one Order Package Binding per captured pair package, one fulfillment case per Order Package Binding, one lab-job effect per fulfillment revision, and one outbox event per aggregate revision/event type. A Commerce Order may contain many pair-level Order Package Bindings. Idempotency records also bind request key to body digest and original result.

## Commerce adapter contract

The adapter is split between a storefront bridge and an authenticated administration adapter. Unsupported capabilities must resolve to an explicit fallback or installation failure; core code does not branch on provider names.

The final contract must additionally cover validation-token materialization and limits, cart package normalization, order edits, additional-payment collection, holds/releases, cancellation and refund normalization, fulfillment splits, capability-version provenance, and plan-tier restrictions. A Financial Adjustment Request is a provider-neutral command with an idempotency key and expected order revision; each adapter returns the authoritative new order revision, affected allocations, amount due or refunded, collection state, and recovery path. Replace ambiguous booleans with strategy enums where behavior differs materially.

Before calling the core platform-neutral, implement an executable fake non-Shopify adapter and run the complete conformance suite against it. A minimal WooCommerce contract spike after the Shopify pilot is the first cross-language proof.

## Proposed implementation structure

The production implementation belongs in the new `vzn-care/vznrx` Agent Native repository. This `agent-native` repository remains the planning and design source. The implementation is a TypeScript monorepo using SQL through Drizzle for governed state and shared actions as the canonical operation surface. It initially deploys to Vercel with an isolated Neon Postgres database and separate development, staging, and production environments. Before production PHI enters either provider, Mission executes and retains the applicable BAAs, enables HIPAA on the exact covered team and projects, documents covered services and shared-responsibility controls, verifies access, audit, and incident procedures, and passes security/privacy review. PHI is prohibited from logs, traces, analytics, build artifacts, schema descriptions, and provider metadata. Shopify extensions and validation functions remain dedicated packages; custom routes are restricted to Shopify OAuth, signed embed sessions, protected uploads, raw webhook verification, and other unavoidable integration boundaries. Protected document bytes reside in a dedicated private Amazon S3 production bucket under an AWS BAA, with public access blocked, short-lived signed access, dedicated KMS encryption, malware-scan quarantine, versioning, access logging, retention, backup, and governed deletion. Neon stores only opaque object references and authorization metadata. Repository creation, access controls, branch protections, environments, CI, deployment ownership, and secret provisioning are explicit blocking issues in Linear milestone M0 rather than hidden prerequisites.

**Owners:** Shopify engineer and backend engineer. **Linear milestones:** M1–M3.

<RichText id="operations-heading" title={"Workstream 5 — Lab fulfillment and operations"}>

## Workstream 5 — Lab fulfillment and operations

**Owners:** fulfillment engineer, optical lead, lab operations, and support lead. **Linear milestones:** M1 vertical slice through M4 operational readiness.

A lab job may be created only when all release predicates are true: the authoritative Shopify order exists; the merchant-approved financial state is satisfied; fraud/risk and cancellation holds are clear; bound commercial lines match the signed plan; the prescription case is approved; the optical configuration is approved; frame/lens availability is confirmed; the latest order edit/refund/cancellation has been processed; and the manufacturing release policy permits work to begin. Otherwise the fulfillment case remains quarantined with an explicit reason.

The architecture represents that financial predicate as Financial Clearance. During the Shopify pilot, an authorized operator confirms it manually against a refreshed authoritative order revision and the system records the approver, time, evidence, and decision. Configurable automated clearance policies are post-pilot scope.

An authoritative payment reversal, fraud hold, chargeback, or comparable Financial Risk Event revokes Financial Clearance and blocks release when received before Production Commitment. After commitment, it opens an audited financial incident but does not automatically interrupt, discard, or rewrite the Production Attempt; the lab completes or safely dispositions the work under policy. The customer optical workflow remains separate from the merchant dispute, and merchant responsibility for properly committed production is governed by the commercial agreement. Exceptional manual risk holds are reason-bound and audited.

Inventory follows two distinct commitments. An authoritative paid-order acceptance creates a SKU-level Frame Reservation while the pair awaits optical readiness. Production Release assigns an inspected Frame Unit and converts that reservation into a Frame Allocation. Merchant-configurable reservation deadlines operate within Mission bounds; expiration creates an explicit extend, substitute, backorder, or cancellation decision and never silently releases stock from a paid obligation. A frame change transfers inventory protection only after compatibility, price, and availability checks succeed.

Paid pairs that still need prescription information, measurements, approvals, or corrections receive configurable reminders before a Customer Action Deadline bounded by Mission policy. Expiration creates an Expired Customer Action Hold, suspends the delivery estimate, and notifies the customer and merchant. The default never auto-cancels a paid order: the merchant explicitly extends the deadline, selects an approved substitution or backorder treatment, or cancels and refunds through the order-owning channel. A future auto-cancellation policy may be configurable only within Mission limits.

Checkout presents a conditional Customer Delivery Estimate with separate customer-action and production-and-shipping phases. The Production SLA Clock begins only at Production Commitment after every optical, financial, frame, and release condition is satisfied. Relevant holds suspend or recalculate the estimate. Lab timing enters through the Lab Adapter and becomes a customer-safe expected ship range. Merchants may configure approved language and conservative buffers but cannot alter authoritative milestone timestamps.

The order-owning commerce platform remains authoritative for shipping destinations. Mission stores a Fulfillment Destination Snapshot at order acceptance and re-fetches it immediately before label purchase. Customer changes normally flow through the merchant channel; an update after Production Release holds shipment until validated. When a platform cannot edit the address, Mission may use an audited manual correction only after verified customer identity and documented merchant authorization. Protected prescription data never appears in commerce address or fulfillment fields.

The HyperLight pilot sends merchant-branded Optical Action Requests by email through Amazon SES in Mission's BAA-covered AWS account. Messages contain no prescription values, measurements, documents, or detailed clinical reasons; they provide only customer-safe status, the requested action, transparent Mission fulfillment identification, and a short-lived signed link. Delivery, bounce, complaint, and suppression events are reconciled. SMS remains disabled until a BAA-covered provider, consent and opt-out model, content policy, and support workflow are approved.

The commerce platform also owns return authorization and financial refunds. Mission inspects returns involving optical quality, defects, remakes, warranties, or production investigations. Prescription lenses never re-enter inventory. A returned Frame Unit may become usable only through a traceable Frame Recovery process covering lens removal, identity-safe handling, inspection, disposition approval, and preserved unit history; otherwise it is quarantined, returned, or responsibly disposed of. Physical receipt never automatically creates a refund, remake, Replacement Credit, or other financial effect.

The pilot requires two distinct identities: an Optical Reviewer grants Optical Approval for the current prescription, measurements, frame, lens, and manufacturing interpretation; a Release Operator grants Production Release after independently confirming Financial Clearance, quote integrity, Frame Allocation, approvals, and holds against those same revisions. Release emits an immutable, content-digested Production Instruction Package bound to one Production Attempt and every exact approved input revision. The lab explicitly accepts or rejects it using an idempotent acknowledgment. Break-glass release is reason-bound and requires retrospective second review.

Every approval records an immutable Approval Basis containing the exact dependent revisions. A consequential input change automatically invalidates all downstream approvals and instructions that depend on it; operators cannot manually carry stale approvals forward. A replacement within a Mission-validated Frame Equivalence Class may preserve Optical Approval but still requires renewed operational release. Merchants may disable or narrow approved equivalence, never broaden it to an unvalidated or customer-visible difference.

Frame Profile Corrections are prospective revisions, never in-place edits. New configurations use the corrected profile; every uncommitted dependent case is automatically revalidated and is quarantined with approvals invalidated when the result is incompatible or materially changed. Production-Committed and completed attempts retain their original profile revision. A correction that exposes a safety or quality concern in committed work opens a traceable incident or recall workflow rather than rewriting production history.

Accepted Production Instruction Packages are never edited in place. Before Production Commitment, a correction requires the lab to acknowledge revocation of the prior package before Mission releases a new package revision. After Production Commitment, corrective work creates a new Production Attempt, remake, or incident workflow. Mission records both its own timestamps and the lab's acknowledgment timestamps; the lab's explicit commitment response establishes the manufacturing cutoff.

Lab connectivity is isolated behind one provider-neutral Lab Adapter contract covering package submission, acceptance or rejection, revocation, Production Commitment, status, QA, shipment, and remake events. API, SFTP or file exchange, and audited manual portal implementations share the same digest, idempotency, acknowledgment, reconciliation, and audit guarantees. The pilot may use a manual adapter, but lab-specific identifiers, statuses, and formats never enter the canonical Rx or fulfillment core.

HyperLight uses a Mission-operated Manual Pilot Queue as its first Lab Adapter. Authorized operators receive immutable Production Instruction Packages and explicitly record acceptance or rejection, revocation, Production Commitment, status, QA, shipment, and remake events. The queue is an audited action surface with the same digest, idempotency, acknowledgment, timestamps, role separation, and reconciliation guarantees as a later automated lab integration; email, spreadsheets, and undocumented portal handoffs are not production authority.

Every Production Attempt receives a QA Inspection under a versioned Mission-controlled Quality Policy. Mission's optical and quality governance process owns the specific numeric and categorical Critical QA Limits and required measurement methods; no pair-level role, merchant, or lab may broaden or waive them. Ordinary policy publication requires both a Mission Optical or Clinical Lead and a Mission Lab or Quality Lead. A narrowly assigned Mission System Administrator may publish any policy or limit change without secondary approval through an Administrative Break-Glass Publication: fresh step-up authentication, stated reason and evidence, policy and affected-case preview, immutable audit, and immediate post-publication notification to optical, quality, and security leads. Notification is not approval and cannot block the change. Merchant administrators never receive this authority. A configured QA Manager may approve only results within documented noncritical tolerance bands, with the check, measured result, allowed tolerance, reason, approver, and attempt recorded. Critical failures require rework, a new attempt, or incident disposition.

Post-payment edits never mutate the original configuration or quote. They create a new revision and require revalidation; cancellation, partial refund, chargeback, frame substitution, production cutoff, and remake transitions are defined in the state machine and runbook.

Direct merchant edits to Mission-bound frame, lens, or service lines invalidate reconciliation and Production Release and quarantine the affected pair. Merchants resolve them through a Mission-controlled change workflow; unrelated order lines remain independently editable.

Every Production Attempt records an idempotent Production Commitment event. Cancellation before commitment guarantees that manufacturing is blocked; cancellation at or after commitment is a best-effort interception governed by the merchant's post-production policy, and delayed events cannot rewrite completed attempt history.

Mission does not directly mutate the channel's financial order. It creates a pair-specific Financial Adjustment Request with the affected components, recommended amount, reason, production state, and permitted automation policy; the order-owning commerce adapter executes or submits the request and returns the authoritative result.

</RichText>

## Post-pilot adapter roadmap

Before selecting adapter two, inventory the actual commerce platforms used by 100–200 qualified Mission prospects. Weight signed demand, GMV, geography, implementation uniformity, and support burden—not global store count alone.

Every candidate receives an Adapter Support Level based on enforceable capabilities. Full adapters require server-side checkout integrity, authoritative order reads, recoverable event processing, financial adjustment handling, and fulfillment updates. Platforms that cannot enforce the package use Mission-hosted checkout or remain non-transactional rather than relying on post-order quarantine as the normal safety mechanism.

1. **WooCommerce** when reachable merchant demand outweighs its plugin/theme/hosting variability.
2. **BigCommerce** when Mission values a cleaner SaaS adapter and mid-market/headless capability.
3. **Commerce Layer** for Mission-owned B2B, headless, spatial, or white-label channels where Mission intentionally owns the transaction path.
4. **Adobe Commerce or Salesforce Commerce** only for an anchor enterprise engagement that funds bespoke packaging and support.
5. **Wix and Squarespace** through the hosted configurator/fallback strategy when qualified demand justifies their more constrained surfaces.

The investment gate for any adapter is three committed design partners or one anchor account that covers build and first-year support.
